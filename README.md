# Job Interview Checkpoint

Answers to the five questions asked by the recruiter, supported with arguments
and concrete examples.

---

## Question 1 — Introduce yourself

Good morning. My name is **Riadh Bakhti** and I am a **full-stack JavaScript
developer**, trained at **GOMYCODE** where I followed the *Full-stack JavaScript
Developer* program.

I started with the fundamentals of the web — HTML, CSS and JavaScript — and then
moved on to modern front-end development with **React** and **Redux**, before
learning the back-end with **Node.js, Express and MongoDB**. I also work with
**Git/GitHub**, REST APIs and agile collaboration methods.

During my training I built several concrete projects: a **TaskFlow** task
management application, a **Redux ToDo** application, a **User Directory**
consuming a REST API with axios, and a larger **ERP** project. These projects
taught me how to structure an application, manage state, consume APIs, debug
problems and deliver a working product.

Beyond the technical side, I am a **curious and rigorous** person. I like
understanding *why* things work, writing clean code and continuously improving
my skills. My goal today is to join a team where I can contribute to real
projects, learn from experienced developers and grow into a confirmed
full-stack engineer.

**Why this introduction works:** it is short (about one minute), it follows a
clear structure — *who I am → what I know → what I have built → what I want* —
and it ends with a professional objective that connects my profile to the
company's needs.

## Question 2 — Three reasons why you should recruit me instead of another candidate

### 1. A solid and practical full-stack technical foundation
I do not only know the theory: I have built and shipped complete applications
with React, Redux, Node.js, Express and MongoDB. I understand the whole chain —
from the user interface to the database and the API in between — which allows me
to be productive quickly and to communicate efficiently with both front-end and
back-end teams.

### 2. Strong problem-solving and learning ability
I am comfortable with unfamiliar problems. When I faced a bug or a technology I
did not know, I learned to isolate the problem, read the documentation, test
hypotheses and find a solution. For example, while building my Redux application,
I had to understand how actions, reducers and the store interact to fix state
updates. This ability to learn fast is, in my opinion, more valuable than
knowing a single framework.

### 3. Reliability, rigour and team spirit
I take my commitments seriously: I respect deadlines, I document my work and I
communicate when something is blocking me. I enjoy working in a team, sharing
knowledge and receiving feedback, because I believe that a good developer is
also a good teammate.

**Conclusion:** I bring technical skills that are immediately useful, the
ability to learn and adapt, and a professional attitude based on responsibility
and collaboration.

---

## Question 3 — Are you responsible?

**Yes, I am responsible — and I can prove it with concrete behaviour.**

- **I respect deadlines.** During my GOMYCODE training, I had to deliver several
  checkpoints and projects in parallel. I organised my work with a task list,
  prioritised the most important deliverables and submitted everything on time.
- **I own my mistakes.** When a project does not work, I do not look for excuses:
  I investigate, fix the problem and learn from it. For instance, when one of my
  submissions was rejected because the project did not match the instructions,
  I rebuilt the application correctly and resubmitted a working version.
- **I think about the consequences of my work.** I write readable code, I test my
  features before delivering them and I document how to run the project, because
  I know that another developer — or the user — will depend on it.
- **I keep my commitments.** If I say I will do something, I do it; and if I
  cannot, I warn the people concerned as early as possible.

Responsibility, for me, is not only about finishing a task: it is about
delivering quality work that others can trust.

## Question 5 — Three of your flaws

### 1. Perfectionism
I sometimes spend too much time polishing details instead of delivering a
working version first.
**What I do about it:** I now work in iterations — I build a functional MVP,
validate it, then improve it. I set myself time limits for each task.

### 2. Difficulty saying "no"
I tend to accept too many tasks because I want to help.
**What I do about it:** I learned to clarify priorities and to communicate my
workload. I use a prioritised task list and I discuss deadlines before
committing.

### 3. Nervousness when speaking in public
Presenting in front of a group is not my natural comfort zone.
**What I do about it:** I prepare and rehearse my presentations, I structure my
message (introduction → arguments → conclusion) and I practise regularly. This
is precisely why I worked on the public-speaking checkpoint: to turn this
weakness into a strength.

**Why this answer is honest and professional:** each flaw is real but is
immediately followed by a concrete improvement strategy, which shows
self-awareness and a growth mindset rather than a simple list of weaknesses.

## Conclusion

These five answers form a coherent professional narrative: a **trained,
project-driven full-stack developer** who is **responsible, motivated and aware
of his areas for improvement**, and who is ready to bring value to a team from
day one.
